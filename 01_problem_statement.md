# Problem Statement

> **Independent portfolio case study based exclusively on publicly available information.**
> This project is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced on behalf of the Government of Alberta, Service Alberta, or Alberta Land Titles and Surveys. It is a self-directed analysis built to demonstrate business analysis, process improvement, and data analytics skills.

## Context

Alberta Land Titles and Surveys processes real estate registrations underpinning more than $340 million in daily economic activity and over $50 billion annually. Between April 2021 and December 2022, submission volumes rose roughly 86%, driving title-transfer processing times to a peak of 84 business days (and survey processing to 55 business days) by December 2022. Through 2023, increased staffing and process changes brought processing back to a stated standard of 10–12 business days, with $10.5 million invested in early work toward a replacement system. The Law Society of Alberta has separately reported that a meaningful share of documents submitted from law offices are flagged with deficiencies, consuming examiner capacity and slowing registration for compliant submitters.

The province is now transitioning users from legacy systems (SPIN2) toward the Alberta Registry for Land Online (ARLO), intended to streamline processes, reduce legacy-system reliance, and improve the search/access experience — with further rollout expected through 2026.

## Business Question

**How can Alberta Land Titles reduce processing delays, document deficiencies, and operational risk while transitioning users from legacy systems to ARLO?**

## Current State

- Large and volatile submission volume tied to housing-market activity
- Recent history of a multi-year backlog (peak 84-day turnaround) driven by a submission surge
- A material share of submissions arrive with document deficiencies, generating rework loops between examiners and submitters
- Dual-system environment: legacy SPIN2 alongside the newer ARLO platform, with users (lawyers, registry agents, the public) operating across both during transition
- Limited public visibility into operational performance beyond point-in-time "currently registering" dates and periodic press releases
- External users — real estate lawyers, registry agents, buyers/sellers, lenders — bear the direct cost of delays (financing gaps, late tax penalties, delayed subdivision approvals)

## Desired State

- Lower document deficiency / rework rate at first submission
- Predictable, stable turnaround time that holds even as volume fluctuates with the housing market
- Higher digital adoption and successful migration of user workflows to ARLO
- Fewer manual interventions in intake and validation
- Better operational reporting — leading indicators of service pressure, not just lagging backlog announcements
- A smooth, low-disruption ARLO transition for external professional users

## Scope of This Case Study

This project analyzes the *service demand and operational risk* side of the problem using public data (Edmonton property assessments, Alberta housing starts, population growth, and publicly reported Land Titles processing-time history) and produces the standard Business Services Analyst deliverable set: process maps, root-cause analysis, requirements, a demand-forecast model, a Power BI dashboard, and a project management package. It does not use or claim access to internal Land Titles operational or transaction-level data, which are not publicly available.
